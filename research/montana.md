# Montana water: research fact sheet (supply, demand, places, law, GIS)

Compiled 2026-11-12 for the Montana chapter of the western-water map. Style follows `colorado.md`.

**Conventions**
- AF = acre-feet; MAF = million AF; cfs = cubic feet per second; DNRC = Montana Department of Natural Resources and Conservation; FWP = Montana Fish, Wildlife & Parks; USBR = Bureau of Reclamation; USACE = Army Corps of Engineers; NRCS = Natural Resources Conservation Service; SWP = Montana State Water Plan 2015; CGWA = controlled groundwater area; CSKT = Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes.
- Evidence tags: **[P]** = I opened the document itself (downloaded the PDF or page and read the number in its text). **[S]** = number seen only in a search-engine summary; re-open before use. **[derived]** = my arithmetic. **unverified** = could not confirm.
- 2026 context: winter 2025-26 was the warmest on record in Montana. High-elevation snow was near normal in places, but low and middle elevations melted a month early. 2026 was the sixth straight year with drought somewhere in the state. Present 2026 readings as that year's readings.

### 1c. Headline set used on the map (one measure: water consumed)
- Supply: almost 44 MAF flows out of Montana a year; 65% of it starts in the state.
- Consumed: about 3.6 MAF/yr. Farms 2.41 MAF (67%), reservoir evaporation 1.00 MAF (28%), everything else 0.17 MAF (5%) [derived from Fig. 11 sums; the plan rounds the last to "approximately 200,000"].
- "Where it comes from" chart: the same consumed water split by river basin (Upper Missouri 33%, Lower Missouri 30%, Clark Fork and Kootenai 19%, Yellowstone 18%) [derived].
- Diversion measure, stated separately: 10.4 MAF/yr diverted for irrigation; 72 MAF/yr passes through hydropower turbines.
