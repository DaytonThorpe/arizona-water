# Arizona water: DEMAND-side fact sheet

Compiled 2026-11-11. Supply side (rivers, reservoirs, groundwater law, climate) is deliberately excluded.

**Conventions**
- AF = acre-feet; MAF = million AF; MGD = million gallons/day; GPCD = gallons per capita per day. 1 AF = 325,851 gal.
- **[derived]** = my arithmetic from sourced numbers (not stated by the source). **[unverified]** = could not confirm in a source I opened; do not publish without checking. **[secondary]** = number attributed to a primary source but only seen via a secondary one.
- All lat/lon values are approximate centroids from general geographic knowledge, NOT taken from a cited source. Spot-check on a map before plotting.
- Many numbers below are company- or industry-reported; flagged where so.

**The "same water as 1957" claim (exact wording)**
- ADWR: "In 1957, the state used approximately 7.1 million acre-feet of fresh water." ... "In 2015, we used 6.9 million acre-feet." Same page: Arizona managed to "increase its population by almost six million citizens and expand its economy by nearly 500 percent." https://www.azwater.gov/node/240
- ADWR: "This is the state, after all, that today is using less water than it did in 1957." https://www.azwater.gov/node/1551
- CAP factsheet (Feb 2023, source "Census, ADWR"): population **+547%** from 1957-2021; water use **-3%** from 1957-2019; "our water use is essentially the same as it was more than half a century ago!" https://library.cap-az.com/documents/factsheets/CAP-Conservation-Factsheet-022123.pdf
- Population basis: ~1.1 million in 1957 vs 6.58 million (EPCOR citing ADWR). https://epcor.com/us/en/az/operations/infrastructure/safe-reliable-water-system/water-efficiency.html
- Caveat: "7x the people" is not what the sources say. CAP's +547% = ~6.5x. With Census July 2025 pop of 7,623,818 it is ~6.9x [derived]. Statewide use actually peaked ~1980 before falling below 1957 levels (2015 state water supply plan, secondary).

- Butler Valley: four State Trust land leases, 3,520 acres; no charge for or reporting of groundwater. Rent example from Auditor General (Feb 2024): paying ~$30/acre where ~$175/acre was market. https://fronterasdesk.org/node/1872195
- Oct 2023: Gov. Hobbs terminated one lease (640 acres, for uncured defaults first noticed 2016) and said the other three would not be renewed at Feb 2024 expiry. https://www.azag.gov/press-release/attorney-general-mayes-issues-statement-fondomonte-leases ; https://ktar.com/arizona-news/arizona-terminates-lease-of-saudi-owned-fondomonte-after-investigation

### 2g. Value vs water
- Ag sales $5.2B (2022 Census). On-farm agriculture = **0.4% of Arizona GDP** while irrigation etc. = ~77% of water use. https://www.extension.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2025-01/Arizona-County-Agricultural-Economy-Profiles_2022.pdf

### 2h. Colorado River shortage and Pinal County
- CAP Ag Settlement Pool: lost 65% of its allocation in 2022 (Tier 1), cut to zero in 2023, still zero in 2024. Arizona's 2022 cut was >300,000 AF, nearly all on CAP. https://azwaterinnovation.asu.edu/impacts-colorado-river-water-shortages-agriculture-central-arizona
- Ag was 90% of CAP demand in the Pinal AMA before the cuts. https://wrrc.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2026-02/Pinal_Factsheet_02.2026.pdf
- Measured effect (ASU remote sensing, Maricopa-Stanfield IDD): cultivated farmland fell ~30% from 2020 to 2024; alfalfa -48%, corn -70%, barley +159%; district water-balance deficit grew from 26,000 AF (2020) to 92,000 AF (2024). Neighboring Ak-Chin (senior tribal rights) stayed ~99% cultivated. (ASU link above.)
- Pre-shortage projections (not outcomes): 54,500-71,100 acres idle once fully cut off; UA model of a 300,000 AF cutback = 75% of county cotton acreage fallowed; "up to 40%" of land fallowed (HCN). https://www.extension.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2024-12/Summary_Statewide-Drought-Impacts.pdf ; https://www.hcn.org/issues/51-4/agriculture-colorado-river-cuts-cloud-central-arizona-farmers-future-drought/
- County-wide fallowed acres actually realized: **unverified**.
- 2026-27 context (supply side, FYI): Sept 2026 reporting says Arizona must cut ~760,000 AF and CAP faces ~50% cut in 2027. https://cronkitenews.azpbs.org/2026/09/11/data-centers-water-colorado-river/ ; https://www.axios.com/local/phoenix/2026/09/21/how-phoenix-is-preparing-for-colorado-river-water-cuts

### 3b. Outdoor share, lawns, pools

## Contested / watch-outs
1. Sector split depends on who counts golf, mining, power and "environmental" demand (72/22/6 ADWR vs 78/21/1 USGS vs 62% ag in ADWR's partial basin reports).
2. "7x the people": sources support ~6.5x (CAP) to ~6.9x (2025 population); the water totals are 7.1 MAF (1957) vs 6.9-7.0 MAF (2015-2017). No ADWR total newer than 2017 was found.
